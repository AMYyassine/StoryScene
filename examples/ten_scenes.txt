Rose discovered a brass key in the garden wall. A folded note beside it named the old archive and warned her to return before dawn.

She brought the key to Lea, who recognized the seal on the note. It belonged to the library where her mother had once worked.

At dusk, the two friends crossed the market. A bookseller remembered Lea's mother and pointed them toward the northern gate.

The gatekeeper refused to let them pass until Rose showed him the seal. He opened a narrow door in the wall and told them to keep the lamp covered.

Beyond the wall, rain had flooded the path. Lea used a fallen branch to test the water while Rose sheltered the lamp beneath her coat.

They reached the archive as its clock struck midnight. Rose gave Lea the brass key, and Lea turned it in the door.

Inside, drawers of letters filled the walls. One drawer carried Lea's name, although she had never visited the archive before.

The letter inside was written by her mother. It asked Lea to choose which family story should be remembered and which should remain private.

Lea read the letter aloud to Rose. Together they decided to bring the account of the lost garden home, leaving the other letters untouched.

Before dawn, they returned through the market. Lea carried the letter, Rose carried the key, and the covered lamp had finally gone out.
